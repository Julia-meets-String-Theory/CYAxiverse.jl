CYAxiverse.minimizer._is_converged_minimum — Method
_is_converged_minimum(result)

Whether a minimize result is a converged Newton run ending at an accepted stationary point (a Newton step within the step tolerance, see "stationary") with a positive smallest computed Hessian eigenvalue (Hsign == 1). Computed saddles and maxima, runs that do not converge, and endpoints whose gradient is below the Optim tolerance only because some instanton scales are small are rejected. The test is heuristic: it does not prove that a minimum is nearby, nor that the minimum is isolated (#220).

CYAxiverse.minimizer.critical_points — Method
critical_points(L, Q; phases=zeros(size(Q, 2)), starts=4096,
                residual_tolerance=1e-10, merge_tolerance=1e-7,
                initial_points=nothing)

Find critical points of an integer-charge cosine potential deterministically in the unit torus. Q has axions in rows and instantons in columns; L[1, :] stores coefficient signs and L[2, :] stores base-10 logarithmic magnitudes.

Initial points are a Halton sequence, so repeated calls are reproducible. Roots are folded into [0,1)^N, deduplicated using periodic distance, and classified by Hessian inertia. Equation residuals and Newton Jacobians are scaled locally by the largest logarithmic amplitude touching each equation, while the classification Hessian is assembled with a symmetric diagonal congruence scaling. Optional initial_points are deterministic torus seeds; negative physical-Hessian modes at those seeds are also sampled to reach displaced minima in highly dimensional problems. The returned coordinates use the paper convention in which the potential arguments are 2π Q'θ + phases.

CYAxiverse.minimizer.minimize — Method
minimize(LV::Vector, QV, x0::Vector)

Minimize the axion potential $V(x) = \sum_a \Lambda_a (1 - \cos(Q_a \cdot x))$ from the initial point x0, where LV holds the linear instanton scales $\Lambda_a$ and QV is the charge matrix. Return the located stationary point in field coordinates.

The gradient tolerance is relative to the largest gradient bound $s_i = \sum_a |\Lambda_a| |Q_{ia}|$, so the run is invariant under a common rescaling of every $\Lambda_a$ in exact arithmetic (bitwise for power-of-two factors, otherwise up to rounding). "converged" is Optim's convergence flag; "stationary" additionally requires a Newton step $\|H^{-1} \nabla V\|_\infty \le \sqrt{\epsilon}$ (the step tolerance of the run), so that a point displaced along a weak direction, including one that mixes the fields, is not stationary. It is false for a constant potential (all $s_i = 0$) and where a computed Hessian eigenvalue is exactly zero. It is a heuristic acceptance test in Float64, not a proof that a critical point is nearby.

Behaviour change

Before this fix the objective and Hessian passed to Newton's method were mis-shaped (the 1×N row cos.(x' * QV) broadcast against LV as N×N), so they were not the derivatives of $V$ and the iteration could stop at spurious points. This affects direct callers of minimize and subspace_minimize. It does not affect legacy vacuum counts stored by the :legacy pipeline search: its only route to the minimizer is the call to subspace_minimize in vacua_id, and vacua_id raises BoundsError before that call (the call would then raise TypeError on its scalar phase, #217). So those counts never depended on this code. The gradient was already correct.

CYAxiverse.minimizer.subspace_minimize — Method
subspace_minimize(L, Q; runs=10_000, phase=zeros(max(collect(size(Q))...)),
                  initial_points=nothing)

Minimize the subspace with runs random initialisations per phase column, or from the explicit initial_points (a vector of start vectors) when given.

Only converged, stationary points with Hsign == 1 are kept (see _is_converged_minimum): saddles, maxima, unresolved endpoints and runs that did not converge are discarded. The origin is always a critical point; it is added only when the smallest eigenvalue of its Hessian Q * Diagonal(L) * Q' is positive, the same test as Hsign == 1 for Newton endpoints.

Caveats of the origin rule
  • A degenerate origin (a zero Hessian eigenvalue) is unclassified and is not returned: the second-order test cannot tell a minimum from a maximum or saddle there (L = (-1, 1/4), Q = [1 2] gives V = -2 sin⁴(x/2), a strict maximum with zero Hessian). This excludes fine-tuned cancellations such as $\sum_a L_a q_a^2 = 0$ and also rank-deficient flat directions, where some field combination does not appear in V (fewer independent charges than fields), even when the origin is a non-strict minimum.
  • The eigenvalues are computed in Float64. For strongly hierarchical L (scales spanning more than about 16 decades) in a basis that mixes them, the small eigenvalues are below roundoff and their sign is not resolved, so a saddle along a suppressed direction can be kept or a minimum dropped. Hsign from minimize has the same limitation.
Behaviour change

Earlier versions kept every Newton endpoint and always added the origin. Together with the corrected objective and Hessian in minimize, this changes the results of direct calls. It does not change legacy vacuum counts stored by the :legacy pipeline search, which never reaches this function: vacua_id raises BoundsError before its call, and the call would then raise TypeError on its scalar phase (#217).
